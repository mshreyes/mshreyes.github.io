A resource on chemicals used in aquaculture and their ecotoxicity

Madgaonkar S.R., Vashishth S., Chivukula N., Garisetti V., Marigoudar S.R., Sharma K.V., Samal A. (2026). Cell Reports Sustainability 3, 100794.

The paper curates 690 chemicals reported in aquaculture literature and combines them with toxicogenomic, ecotoxicity, regulatory, environmental-fate and food-web data in a database called ReCAnt.

Regulatory coverage

Only 57 of the 690 chemicals carry any regulatory information. The authors caution that absence of information does not mean a chemical is unregulated, and that roughly half are natural products often outside conventional frameworks.

Ecotoxicity (ECOTOX)

ECOTOX gave toxicity data for 142 chemicals across 53 aquaculture-relevant species. Chronic medians are lower than acute ones.

2.30 ppmoverall acute median (119 chemicals, 48 species)
0.48 ppmoverall chronic median (112 chemicals, 49 species)
86.74 L/kgmedian BCF (24 chemicals, 16 species)

Copper sulfate had the most records for both toxicity types, followed by sodium hypochlorite (acute) and atrazine (chronic). Via CTD, atrazine had the most gene associations (4,866); chlorpyrifos had the most phenotype (96) and disease (57) associations.

Of the 37 chemicals with PBMT classifications, all were PMT; six (azithromycin, sulfadiazine, erythromycin, atrazine, triclosan, diuron) were also reported as vPvM.

Food-web trophic transfer

GloBI predator–prey links were joined with ECOTOX BCF and toxicity data. A chemical–predator path is a proxy for potential trophic transfer, not a measured exposure. Filtered networks drop prey that are sensitive (below HC05), a more conservative view.

Chemical space

The 339 structure-resolved compounds are structurally diverse. These analyses are exploratory and descriptive, not mechanistic.

Quinone (26 chemicals) and benzene (24) are the most frequent scaffolds; the next is 6. The quinone scaffold has no known regulatory status in the data, while benzene is the only scaffold shared across the regulatory, therapeutic and toxic-effect profiles.

Limitations

  • The query may miss chemicals described with class-specific terms rather than umbrella terms in titles or abstracts.
  • Only peer-reviewed, PubMed-indexed articles were curated; agency reports and industry documents were excluded.
  • Structures were available for about half the chemicals; mixtures and others rely on manually curated data only.
  • Biotransformation and partitioning values are computational predictions. The case study (atrazine, ivermectin, netilmicin) is not a validation of the broader network.
  • The trophic transfer analysis is a screening demonstration, not a predictive model of transfer or ecological risk.
