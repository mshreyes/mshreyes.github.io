Computational AOPs for organic mercury toxicity in fish

Madgaonkar S.R., Chivukula N., Garisetti V., Marigoudar S.R., Sharma K.V., Samal A. (2026). Environmental Toxicology and Chemistry 45(3), 731–747.

Adverse outcome pathways (AOPs) are slow to develop by hand. This study builds computational AOPs (cAOPs) from existing data: curated toxicity endpoints for 124 organic mercury compounds, molecular docking against zebrafish proteins, and event relationships from AOP-Wiki and the literature, then filters network paths against AOP definitions. It proposes four novel cAOPs.

Compounds, endpoints and targets

Organic mercury compounds and fish-specific data were compiled from CTD and ECOTOX, and checked against 317 curated AOPs from AOP-Wiki.

24fish protein targets (14 CTD, 11 ECOTOX)

AOP-Wiki (October 2024) held 487 AOPs with 1,485 key events (KEs) and 2,035 key event relationships (KERs). A complete, connected subset of 317 curated AOPs (1,023 KEs, 1,573 KERs) was used as the reference.

Docking to find molecular initiating events

Because the 24 targets came from many fish species, zebrafish orthologs were found and docked with AutoDock Vina. A compound counted as a hit if its predicted binding energy was equal to or lower than the reference ligand's and its pose sat in the predicted pocket.

16 zebrafish proteins docked successfully with 44 organic mercury compounds. None of these binding events matched existing molecular initiating events (MIEs) in AOP-Wiki, so they were added as 16 novel MIEs. The authors present them descriptively, as potential interactions rather than established toxic consequences, because most structures are predicted.

Building the toxicity network

Endpoints were standardized to triplets of AOP-Wiki KE, Gene Ontology term and MeSH identifier, then linked by KERs and literature evidence. Triplets sharing an identifier were merged into supernodes to remove redundancy. Select a stage.

Node- and edge-level criteria

Novelty score

Novelty = 1 − the largest share of a path's KERs that overlap any single curated AOP. Paths were ranked by it, and the four most novel became the candidate cAOPs. They form a network of 12 supernodes (4 without a KE) and 11 superedges, 5 of them novel links.

The four proposed cAOPs

All four share the same intermediate events and differ only in the starting event (GPx binding or altered metallothionein levels) and the final outcome (neurologic manifestations or dysbiosis). Select one to see its chain and the weight of evidence (WoE) for each link.

Evidence for each link

Of the five novel links, three had Moderate WoE and two (liver injury to dysbiosis, and liver injury to neurologic manifestations) had Low WoE. Each cAOP had a cumulative WoE of High, meaning at least half its KERs were rated High. Several of the cited fish studies used other stressors, consistent with the stressor-agnostic nature of AOPs; the authors note that the mechanisms were also observed with other stressors.

Docking example: glutathione peroxidase (GPx)

26 compounds bound zebrafish GPx. Hydrargaphen (CID 16683105) formed hydrogen bonds with Tyr9, Glu162 and Glu164; the reference ligand (BDB 51551531) with Tyr9 and Lys122. More negative means stronger predicted binding.

Limitations stated by the authors

  • Endpoints came only from curated toxicology databases, which may miss relevant data.
  • Docking used predicted zebrafish structures and should be interpreted with caution.
  • The approach relies on AOP-Wiki, where many AOPs are under development and not endorsed.
  • Endpoint harmonization and literature mining without organism filters needed substantial manual review, limiting automation.
  • Even with Bradford-Hill-based WoE, causality for the KERs is hard to establish. The cAOPs need experimental validation before submission to AOP-Wiki.
  • The method's applicability depends on the availability and quality of mechanistic data in public databases.
