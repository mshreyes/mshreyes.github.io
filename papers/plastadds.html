Stressor-centric AOP networks for plastic additives

Sahoo A.K., Chivukula N., Madgaonkar S.R., Ramesh K., Marigoudar S.R., Sharma K.V., Samal A. (2024). Archives of Toxicology 98, 3299–3321.

Plastic additives are not covalently bound and can leach out over a plastic's life cycle. The study curates 6,470 plastic additives, links 1,287 of them to 322 adverse outcome pathways (AOPs) using five toxicogenomic and endpoint resources, and explores human and aquatic toxicity pathways for benzo[a]pyrene, bisphenol A (BPA) and DEHP.

Only 4,309 additives could be placed in any of the ten priority use sectors. Synthetic textiles had just 6 additives and no human biospecimen detections.

Regulatory and hazard flags

The 154 EDCs (endocrine-disrupting chemicals, from DEDuCT) and 101 potential carcinogens (IARC) are among the high-production-volume additives.

Linking additives to key events

Endpoints were mapped to key events (KEs) in AOP-Wiki, giving 688 KEs for 1,314 additives. Cytotoxicity-associated bursts in ToxCast were excluded before mapping. Toggle the view.

The plastic additives–AOP network

An additive is linked to an AOP if it shares at least one KE with it. Links are graded by relevance: Level 1 (a KE), 2 (an AO), 3 (an MIE) or 5 (an MIE and AO joined by a directed path). Level 4 never occurs, since it always meets Level 5.

AOPs were classified by outcome into 26 disease classes using the Disease Ontology; 125 of 322 could not be classified. For stricter relevance (Level 5, coverage ≥ 0.4), 107 additives link to 88 AOPs through 526 links. Twenty additives are linked to every KE (coverage 1.0) in 15 AOPs, links not otherwise documented in AOP-Wiki.

Additives with the most highly relevant AOPs

Benzo[a]pyrene is annotated as an additive in the UNEP report, but the authors note evidence that it is more likely a contaminant or byproduct (from extender oils or carbon black). Of these 15 additives, 14 are documented EDCs and 10 are IARC carcinogens. AOPs 263, 264, 265, 267 and 268 are shared by all 15; they share one MIE (decreased coupling of oxidative phosphorylation) and one AO (decreased growth).

Case studies: three well-known pollutants

For each chemical, highly relevant AOPs were assembled into an AOP network, and one human and one aquatic pathway were explored against published evidence. Pathways are potential routes assembled from the literature.

Limitations stated by the authors

  • The UNEP functional annotations can be inaccurate (text mining lacked context sensitivity), so some chemicals may be misclassified as additives.
  • Only 4,309 of 6,470 additives could be placed in use sectors, and only 1,287 could be linked to AOPs, because exposure studies are scarce.
  • The data are mostly mammalian, which limits exploration of ecotoxicological events; 197 of the 322 AOPs capture human-relevant outcomes.
  • Network links show associations through shared KEs, not demonstrated causation.
  • Future work could use larger curated chemical lists, more gene, pathway and species data, and non-human endpoints.
