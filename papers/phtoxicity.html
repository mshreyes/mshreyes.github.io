Network-based investigation of petroleum hydrocarbon ecotoxicity and risk

Sahoo A.K., Madgaonkar S.R., Chivukula N., Karthikeyan P., Ramesh K., Marigoudar S.R., Sharma K.V., Samal A. (2024). Environment International 194, 109163.

Hazard from oil contamination is usually judged from total petroleum hydrocarbon concentrations. This study instead looks at 320 individual petroleum hydrocarbons (PHs), linking them to adverse outcome pathways (AOPs), to species through toxicity and bioconcentration data, and to hazard thresholds and risk quotients for the US EPA priority PAHs.

Presence in chemical regulation lists, by hydrocarbon type

More than 100 of the PHs occur in each of two fuels, crude oil and gasoline. In consumer-product data (CPDat), 54 PHs fall in 17 product-use categories and 61 have reported functional uses, with more than 20 used as solvents and as fragrance components.

Stressor-AOP network

An AOP links a molecular initiating event (MIE) through key events (KEs) to an adverse outcome (AO). PHs were mapped to AOPs where a KE matched their ToxCast, CTD or ECOTOX data. No new AOPs were built, and none of these PH-AOP links were previously in AOP-Wiki.

KEs identified, by data source

CTD KEs come from phenotypes (49, linked to 18 PHs) and diseases (35 KEs from 54 diseases, 15 PHs). ToxCast: 56 PHs had relevant endpoints, 19 mapped to KEs.

Links by level of relevance

Relevance levels: 1, a KE only; 2, an AO; 3, an MIE; 5, both MIE and AO with a directed path between them. Level 4 does not occur, since any link meeting its criteria also meets Level 5. The priority PAHs have 975 links to 171 AOPs.

Case study: benzo[a]pyrene

B[a]P is linked to 169 AOPs, more than any other PH here, and is the only PH with a coverage score of 1 (all KEs) in two AOPs (AOP 30 and AOP 263).

Network hubs in the directed B[a]P network

In-degree (KE 317, altered cardiovascular development/function, and AO 1193, breast cancer): 5. Out-degree (MIE 18, AhR activation): 12. 51 of the 92 KEs (9 MIEs, 13 AOs) are associated with B[a]P through the integrated data. The AO apoptosis (KE 1262) has the highest betweenness; AhR activation has the highest eccentricity.

The authors used AOP 341 (DNA methyltransferase inhibition leading to transgenerational effects, Moderate weight of evidence, relevant to Daphnia magna) to lay out published evidence on B[a]P transgenerational effects. The evidence they cite is mixed: effects in medaka were primarily mediated by histone modifications and miRNAs rather than DNA methylation. The result is a hypothesized pathway, not a validated one.

Which species are affected

Bipartite stressor-species networks built from ECOTOX acute toxicity (LC50/EC50, minimum value per pair) and bioconcentration factors (maximum value per pair).

Hazard thresholds (HC05)

Species sensitivity distributions give HC05, the concentration hazardous to 5% of species; lower means more toxic. Model-averaged values are shown for the US EPA SSD Toolbox. The R package ssdtools gave similar results. Priority PAHs are highlighted. Log scale, ppm.

Acute SSDs needed LC50/EC50 data at 24–96 h from at least 5 ECOTOX species groups (14 PHs qualified); chronic SSDs used NOEC (6 PHs). The PNEC is the chronic HC05 divided by an assessment factor of 5. Both acute and chronic orderings of the PAHs follow the number of benzene rings, which the authors note as a possible link between ring count and toxicity. Crustaceans, fish and molluscs commonly sit in the sensitive region of the distributions.

Risk quotients in Indian waters

RQ is the measured environmental concentration divided by the PNEC; RQ ≥ 1 indicates high ecological risk. Concentrations came from 8 studies (253 Scopus records screened). Select a PAH.

Log scale. Orange: RQ ≥ 1. The Mithi river had the highest risk for all four PAHs; B[a]P also exceeded 1 in the Mahanadi and Gomti rivers and at Veraval harbour. Thane creek, Simsang, and Kharkai and Subarnarekha rivers were low risk.

Limitations

  • The PH list may be incomplete because analytical methods cannot fully resolve fuel oil composition.
  • Many AOP-Wiki AOPs lack taxonomic annotation, so the ecotoxicologically relevant set is incomplete.
  • Some HC05 values have wide confidence intervals; more stressor-specific toxicity data across species are needed.
  • Thresholds may not apply to a specific water body because test-location information was sparse.
  • Mixture effects could not be analyzed.
